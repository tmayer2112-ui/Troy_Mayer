About

Mechanism, signal, and the model in between.

I'm Troy Mayer — a Mechanical Engineering and Applied Statistics student at Purdue with a minor in Economics. The two degrees aren't a coincidence: most of the engineering problems I find interesting are the ones where you can't separate the hardware from the data it produces.

That's been the through-line. At Boston Dynamics I characterize impact events on Atlas — decomposing force vectors to localize loads, filtering the signal, and fitting damped sinusoidal models to pull out natural frequency, damping ratio, and absorbed energy. At Purdue ASME I write the real-time C++ firmware that closes a 500 Hz loop from EMG electrode to actuator, and I routed the PCB it runs on. In between, I've built LSTM forecasters for a Caterpillar paint line and LabVIEW acquisition rigs for soft-robotics gels.

Right now I'm most interested in legged robotics and state estimation — enough that I've been building a contact-aided error-state EKF for a simulated quadruped on my own time, mostly to understand observability by breaking it myself.

Contact-Aided State Estimation for a Legged Robot

C++ · Eigen · MuJoCo · GoogleTest

The Problem

A legged robot has no direct sensor for where its body is. IMU integration drifts within seconds, and there is no GPS indoors. But every time a foot is planted, that contact point is momentarily fixed in the world — which is a measurement, if you model it correctly.

What I Built

A contact-aided error-state extended Kalman filter in C++/Eigen (with a Python reference) for a simulated Unitree Go1. It propagates on the IMU, adds each planted foot to the state at touchdown, corrects with forward kinematics while the foot is down, and marginalizes it at liftoff.

Results

  • Velocity RMSE 0.013 m/s against MuJoCo ground truth over a 60-second walk (mean of 10 sensor-noise seeds), against 1.38 m/s for IMU-only dead reckoning.
  • Runs at 1 kHz, one update per IMU sample; the C++ step takes about 20 µs of the 1 ms budget.
  • Jacobians verified against finite differences in GoogleTest; the C++ filter matches the Python reference to 1e-13 on a recorded walk.
  • IMU noise and bias terms taken from the BMI088 data sheet, not tuned.

Observability Finding

With only an IMU and leg kinematics, roll, pitch, and velocity stay bounded while position and yaw drift without limit: gravity is the only absolute direction the sensors can see. Derived analytically, then confirmed numerically (exactly four unobservable directions in every window tested). The weakest observable direction is tilt against accelerometer bias, which only separates once the robot turns.

What Breaks It

  • About 2 cm/s of foot slip doubles the velocity error; the per-sample χ² gate never catches a smooth slide.
  • Force-only contact detection loses yaw fast as latency grows; ANDing with the gait schedule removes the cliff.
  • A tutorial-style Q = 0.01·I loses attitude entirely; data-sheet Q doesn't.

Electromechanical Optimization of Ionic Dielectric Gels

Undergraduate Researcher · Soft Adaptive Systems & Structures

Summary

Maximizing the electromechanical force output of ionic dielectric gels for soft robotic actuators, through targeted chemical formulation paired with a custom automated test stack.

Two Ways to Measure

Flat samples sit under a Keyence laser displacement head, which reads sub-millimeter motion directly into LabVIEW. A cantilever is a different problem: the tip sweeps through an arc and leaves the laser's line, so there is nothing for it to track.

For those I wrote a Python computer-vision autotracker that resolves tip displacement frame by frame against a micrometer scale in shot — covering the geometry the laser could not.

Approach & Results

  • Built LabVIEW and MATLAB data acquisition systems, processing and normalizing datasets of 50,000+ points.
  • Iterated gel formulations for a force output increase of over 50%.
  • Designed 15 custom Fusion 360 test fixtures to standardize the testing workflow.
  • Achieved 0.01 mm tracking precision on a 29.45 mm cantilever sample.
